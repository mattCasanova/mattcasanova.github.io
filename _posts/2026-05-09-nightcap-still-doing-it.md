Saturday, May 9. I'm working. Friday: working. Last Saturday: working. Last Sunday: working. Tomorrow: probably working.

Two weekends running, both days.

A few days ago I [shipped the post](/2026/05/06/bad-advice-prove-them-wrong/) where I linked an eight-year-old YouTube video of me walking into the office at 4 AM on a Saturday morning after the gym. I said *eight years on, I'm still doing it.*

This is the receipt for that sentence.

The math I keep coming back to: **how much would you work for double the pay? For triple?** Pick whatever number ends the conversation in your head — that's the number that should let you put down a Saturday without flinching.

Big-tech comp is, roughly, that number.

Zuckerberg isn't dumb. The CEOs of Amazon, Uber, the rest of them — none of them are dumb either. If they're paying you a lot of money, the value bet is real, and the bet runs both ways. You collect a number that closes most arguments. They collect more output than they paid for. **That's the contract.** People want to make $100K, $200K, $300K, $400K — and then the moment they start making it, they treat it as the new baseline and get reluctant to do the work that justified the bet in the first place. The number is supposed to be the receipt for the hours, not a refund of them.

Here's the part I think people forget.

When you were in college, you were *paying* to work 80 hours a week. I used to tell my students: for every hour you spend in class, plan three hours outside. Most weeks they'd still feel underwater. Tuition was real money — six figures by the end — and they handed it over for the privilege of doing assignments nobody outside their class would ever look at.

Now somebody is paying *you* to do work that real customers will actually use. The calendar is the same. The hours are the same. The only thing that flipped is the direction the money flows.

The other half I keep forgetting to count: the work is also paying me in *skill*. New domain, new patterns, real production complexity. Same loop as Vegas — hour for skill, skill for receipts — just at a different altitude. And honestly, the stuff I'm working on right now is genuinely cool. I like it. I'm getting more dangerous with AI every week. I'm improving. None of that shows up on a paystub, but it lands on a resume.

Compare that to the alternative: *find a new job.* Recruiter outreach. Leetcode prep. Behavioral loops. System design. Months of pipeline. Then you maybe land somewhere new, with all-new context to learn, all-new politics to read, all-new chances to fall behind in.

Saturday vs. that? Saturday wins.

Casualty list: no Flux this week. No LiquidMetal2D. No side-project app code. No anything I can show you, actually — the work I *am* doing is the kind I can't post screenshots of. There's a builder-pattern thing coming together that I'd love to write up, and I'll probably try to get a *House Rules: Builder* post out tomorrow morning. Pattern only, no codebase context. But it's interesting and it's 100% what's currently in my head.

For now: capacity is finite, the blog can wait, the engine can wait, the Saturday can't.

It's past midnight. Three whiskies in. The drink is for closing time.

See you, space cowboy.
