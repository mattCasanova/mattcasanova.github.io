I'm making a game.

I've said the opposite on this blog more than once. [I build game engines more than I build games](/2026/09/03/the-unposted-rule/), and I've never had a strong desire to ship a game. That was true for years, for two reasons.

One, I never have an idea for a game.

Two, I'm not an artist.

Both of those changed in the last couple of weeks.

The engine kept stalling for the same reason. I'd go weeks without touching LiquidMetal2D, and the nightcaps kept reporting it: [it's waiting, and it knows I know](/2026/07/06/nightcap-no-fireworks/), then [still parked and still waiting](/2026/07/13/nightcap-lets-crank-it-up/). Meanwhile I keep writing that [the engineering gets cheaper and cheaper](/2026/09/30/cheaper-and-cheaper/) and that [the art has to live somewhere else](/2026/09/30/wheres-the-art/). For a game, the art is whether it's fun. Nobody has ever called a game good because of its skeletal animation.

So: a game.

### The games I keep going back to

I like stealth games.

Tenchu: Stealth Assassins, going all the way back to the PlayStation. That game was great.

Mark of the Ninja, obviously.

The Arkham games, which are really two games in one: the combat is awesome, and the predator stealth is awesome, and they feel great for completely different reasons.

Splinter Cell, all of them, Blacklist especially. I dominated that game. I beat it so many times.

And [Ronin](https://store.steampowered.com/app/274230/RONIN/).

Ronin is a 2D turn-based action game. The core mechanic is the jump: you plan an arc, you commit, and the room plays out one turn at a time while you dodge bullets in midair. There's a grappling hook and you swing on it, and all of it is turn-based. The mechanic is the whole pitch, and the mechanic is great.

I played it years ago. A couple of years back I tried to install it again on my Mac and it wouldn't run.[^thirtytwo]

So there's a game I really like that I can't play anymore.

I also always thought it would be cool in a Batman kind of world. Detective, gadgets, a hero working a room. Except Batman doesn't kill, and in Ronin you absolutely do.

Also, I don't own the rights to Batman.

### Saying the word

So I'm doing a rip-off of Ronin.

I'm using that word on purpose, and here's why I'm comfortable with it. Tomasz Wacławek, who made Ronin, called his own game a rip-off of Gunpoint when he started building it. Gunpoint is Tom Francis's stealth game from 2013. Francis played Ronin and wrote that it clearly wasn't a rip-off, and he meant that as praise.

I've never played Gunpoint. So it's a chain: Gunpoint, then Ronin, then me.

What I think is fair to take is the mechanics and the genre conventions. The levels, the art, the characters, the writing: those have to be mine.

What the game actually is, I'll get into later.

### The art problem

The second reason I never made a game is that I can't draw. My wife is an artist, but she isn't a 2D animation artist, and that's a specific trade.

Here's what changed. Claude edits photos now. It can make pieces of video: if I need a countdown timer to drop into a clip, there are probably other ways to get one, but I'm already paying for Claude and Claude can make it. So over the last couple of days I've been talking through this game and asking whether it can make art assets.

So far, apparently, it can.

That also changes the math on animation. Everything I've shipped before used sprite animation: a separate drawing for every frame of every move. That's a lot of art, and every frame has to stay consistent with the one before it or the character shimmers. That's a bad deal for a guy who can't draw working with a machine that has never drawn the same face twice.

Skeletal animation flips it. You draw the pieces once, an arm, a torso, a head. You build a skeleton of bones and pin the pieces to it. Then you animate the bones and the engine interpolates everything between. The art doesn't change between frames, because it's the same art. Only the bones move.

I taught this years ago and never built it into this engine until this month. And [I've already said I didn't really write it](/2026/09/30/wheres-the-art/), so I won't claim it twice.

### Where it runs

Ronin's jump arc doesn't work well on a phone. Planning a precise arc with your thumb parked on top of the thing you're aiming at is not a good time. An iPad might be fine, with the extra size, and I might try that later.

Which means the thing that's been sitting in my engine plan for a while is finally happening: **LiquidMetal2D is coming to macOS**, with keyboard and mouse support.

So I'm making a Mac game.

### The second engine

On the side, I'm also building a port of LiquidMetal2D on **Vulkan** instead of Metal.

Quick version if you're not a graphics person. Metal is Apple's graphics API, the one LiquidMetal2D talks to when it wants the GPU to draw. Vulkan is the cross-platform successor to OpenGL and it runs basically everywhere. Conceptually the two are close cousins. OpenGL was one big global state machine: every time you changed a setting, the driver had to re-validate things at draw time, and all that checking cost you performance. Metal and Vulkan make you build your pipelines up front. Everything gets validated once, when you create it, and at runtime you just switch between pipelines that are already known to be good. The checking moves from every frame to startup.

That engine is C++, which means I'm going back to C++ on purpose, for fun.

It's called **Dilithium2D**.

Vulkan, so Star Trek. It writes itself, and it's in line with my goofy project names. When I was teaching at DigiPen in Korea I built a 2D C++ engine, the predecessor to Mach 5, and I called it the Warp Engine, because the whole point was making games fast. My freshmen built their first games on it. Dilithium is the thing that makes the warp engine go. The rest of the Trek jokes are already living in the codebase, and they're getting their own post.

Two engines in parallel: LiquidMetal2D for the game, Dilithium2D for fun. And if Dilithium2D gets far enough, the game can run on Linux too. I've been playing with [Omarchy](https://omarchy.org/), DHH's Linux setup.

It has a Tokyo Night theme, by the way.

I like it a lot. Since I do all my programming on a Mac, there's no real reason for me to use it. Unfortunately. But I'd like my game to run there.

The game is going to be free, basically. It isn't a business. It's the thing the engine has been missing for six years.

I've obviously [bitten off more than I can chew](/2026/04/12/define-interesting/) again. A game, a macOS port, and a second engine in a language I haven't shipped in years.

I like staying busy.

I'll keep posting updates as it comes together, and hopefully there are playable demos in the next few weeks. Then I'll tell you what the game actually is.
