This is basically a follow-up, and it comes down to one question: what's the thing I'm actually contributing, me personally?

Three things this week, and they all feel related.

### The bus

In [Phoning It In](/2026/09/28/phoning-it-in/) I built two features and a blog post on a bus from Daegu to Busan. I fully claim that I built those.

But the HIIT timer isn't a hard feature. The art was the where, not the what. It was how I did it: a Mac mini at home that never sleeps, four sessions waiting in their repos, a phone driving them from the other side of the planet, a reviewer on every branch, and nothing merging until I read it on a laptop. The workflow was the quote-unquote genius part.

### The skeleton

Then in [Cheaper and Cheaper](/2026/09/30/cheaper-and-cheaper/) I mentioned that I implemented skeletal animation in my game engine, but it didn't really feel like I did it. Which is still true.

I knew what to do. But it's very specifically a mathematical problem. Here's the math. There's no art to that.

Maybe that used to be bragging rights. Even for a personal engine, there were certainly bragging rights in 2005, or 2010, or 2016 to 2020. Even up to 2024 it was still cool if you did it yourself (obviously there are commercial engines that will do it for you). But skeletal animation in a 2D game engine in September 2026? That's table stakes. Especially considering I didn't even write it.

Same with the perf wins. They're always cool. At this point they're not even my perf wins.

### The blog

The post up right now is, call it, a second draft.

I dictate to Claude through Superwhisper and have it organize and clean up my narration. But I also had it read my blog's inspiration file, which is full of stuff about bars and pop culture... shit like that. And my old posts, to get a feel for the voice. Which is exactly what I told the skill to do.

The first draft opened with a whole intro story I never dictated. It had ideas in it that I didn't contribute.

I didn't like it.

The flip side is that I narrate, but I don't do a good job of getting my ideas out. Claude can clean up my words. If it supplies the ideas, it's not my post.

### The art

The through line through all of this is the art.

I've talked about it here [before](/2026/04/19/no-map-no-magic-prompt/): in *Linchpin*, Seth Godin talks about [giving gifts and doing art](/2026/05/06/bad-advice-prove-them-wrong/). Putting out AI slop is certainly not art.

A big part of that book is that finding people to do the work has been getting cheaper and cheaper. It was written pre-AI, but finding people to move your bricks, or stomp the roaches, or do the copy editing was cheaper and cheaper even back in 2010, when it came out. Now it's incredibly cheap.

But that work is not art. It isn't going to get you noticed. It might need to get done, but it isn't linchpin work.

Which is why I keep saying the barrier to entry has never been lower, but [the bar has never been higher](/2026/09/03/the-unposted-rule/). That's exactly where we stand. The important part is no longer the engineering.

### The game

Which is why I'm working on a game.

We have to wait and see when it comes to any game. But the art of a game should be that it's fun, or that it has cool moments. Not that it has skeletal animation.

It fits the other through line I've been talking about: engineering versus product. In today's world, the engineering just keeps getting cheaper and cheaper.

See you, space cowboy.
